We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled ``\textbf{Brain-Inspired 4-DoF Localization-Mapping with Hippocampal--Entorhinal Spatial Representations}'' for consideration in \emph{Knowledge-Based Systems}.

\vspace{0.6em}

Autonomous localization today is dominated by black-box learning pipelines whose failures are hard to diagnose and whose resource demands are hard to justify. Our manuscript develops an interpretable alternative grounded in explicitly represented spatial knowledge: a brain-inspired localization-mapping framework (NeuroLocMap) that couples dual-stream visual processing with vestibular IMU fusion on a 10-state EKF core, represents space through 3D grid cells and multilayered head direction cells, and stores spatial experience in a multilayer cognitive map whose topology is directly inspectable. On top of this core, two lightweight learned corrections --- a scale-only calibration head over a frozen monocular depth foundation model and a learned-embedding loop-closure line --- are trained once on simulation and deployed without per-domain retraining; we report where they do and do not transfer across the sim-to-real boundary instead of claiming universal zero-shot success.

\vspace{0.6em}

We believe this work fits the scope of \emph{Knowledge-Based Systems} for the following reasons:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Explicit knowledge representation for spatial reasoning.} The experience map is a symbolic-topological knowledge structure (nodes, links, re-anchoring events) built on top of subsignal processing, making localization decisions auditable rather than opaque.
    \item \textbf{Knowledge acquisition with minimal supervision.} All learned components are trained once in simulation and transferred to the real world zero-shot; the resulting sim-to-real gap is measured and reported, including the settings in which transfer degrades.
    \item \textbf{Verifiable, reproducible claims.} Every headline number is evaluated under two alignment calibers (shape-only Sim(3) and rigid SE(3)), with datasets, trained weights, and evaluation scripts released in a public repository.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.6em}

The full system outperforms a 10-state EKF visual-inertial baseline on all five evaluation scenarios (four simulated urban routes and the real-world KITTI 07 sequence) under both calibers, runs at 39--76 FPS on CPU (measured, no GPU), reduces terminal displacement on a dedicated closed-loop benchmark from 842.54~m to 55.92~m, and produces an interpretable topological experience map as a by-product of localization.
